Eligibility Verification Intelligence System

A student-developed healthcare operations case study exploring how a small intake defect can become a visible eligibility exception—and how an earlier verification control could interrupt that path.

Integrity boundary

Student-developed. Simulated. No PHI.

No patient information, protected health information, employer data, payer data, claims data, EHR data, or real eligibility transactions are used. All rates, rework counts, and comparison outcomes are modeled educational values—not real-world healthcare performance results.

Core operational insight

Eligibility exceptions can be downstream signals of earlier intake defects. The operational question is not only what failed—but where the workflow first lost control.

01

Problem

A small demographic error can enter registration quietly and become visible only when eligibility verification produces an exception.

Simulated event

DOB transposition

A fictional date of birth is entered incorrectly during registration. The defect is small at the point of entry, but it can create additional investigation and rework when it reaches eligibility.

Visible signal

Eligibility exception

The eligibility response is treated as a signal that the workflow needs investigation—not automatically as the original source of the problem.

03

Control Design

The redesigned workflow separates prevention, detection, correction, and workflow gating.

01

Structured verification

Preventive control. Confirm critical demographic and insurance information before eligibility verification.

02

Eligibility verification

Detective control. Treat a failed or unexpected response as an early signal requiring investigation.

03

Exception gate

Workflow control. Do not allow an unresolved exception to move silently into later workflow steps.

04

Correct + reverify

Corrective control. Validate the source data, correct the simulated defect, and recheck before appropriate downstream progression.

06

What I Learned

The exception was not necessarily the original problem. It was evidence that the workflow may have lost control earlier.

I originally looked at the eligibility mismatch as the problem to correct. This simulation pushed me to separate the failure, the signal, and the control—and to trace the visible exception back to the first point where inaccurate information entered the workflow.

SignalEligibility exception
ControlVerify, investigate, resolve
