## Core Insight — Detection ≠ Resolution

An eligibility workflow can successfully detect a coverage, benefit, authorization, referral, demographic, payer-selection, coordination-of-benefits, or patient-responsibility issue and still fail operationally.

The risk can shift from detection to exception management.

Once an exception is identified, the modeled workflow needs a clear mechanism for documenting the issue, assigning ownership, defining the next action, monitoring follow-up, escalating unresolved items, and confirming closure before the case proceeds.

The operational question is therefore not only:

> “Was eligibility verified?”

It is also:

> “What happened to every exception the verification process uncovered?”

## Interview Talking Point

One concept I have been exploring through my simulated healthcare operations projects is the difference between detecting an exception and actually resolving it.

For example, an eligibility verification process might identify a coverage, benefit, referral, authorization, demographic, payer-selection, coordination-of-benefits, or patient-responsibility issue. Finding it is important, but that alone does not protect the next step in the workflow.

I would want the process to show what the exception was, who owns the next action, what follow-up is required, whether escalation is needed, and whether resolution was confirmed before the case advances.

That has changed how I look at workflow problems. I am learning to ask not only where an issue was found, but where ownership or control may have been lost after it was found.

## EVIS Workflow Rule

**Eligibility Exception Closure Rule™**

An eligibility exception is not considered resolved when it is identified. It is resolved only after the exception is documented, assigned to clear ownership, acted upon, and its closure is confirmed before the workflow advances.

**Detect → Document → Assign → Act → Verify → Close → Advance**

## Portfolio Takeaway

The first control failure may not be the original eligibility problem. It may occur after the problem was correctly identified but before anyone ensured it was resolved.
